Reflection: what the Problem 9 run taught me

Question 1How would I evaluate the agents' performance on each ticket, and why?

Overall the team was fast, followed a sensible order of operations, and left the shop in a correct state: all three tickets resolved, every dollar human-approved, and the ending balance of $152.00 matching cash_accounts exactly (Cash tab). Timing was quick with no hold-ups: 7.5 s, 4.7 s and 6.3 s per ticket, and zero guardrail blocks.

TicketGradeWhy
101 · TeeB+Correct core work: Inventory found 0 in size S and spotted that invoice 501 blocked Bulldog Print Co; Accounting queued invoice 501 ($840) and Customer Service wrote an honest draft to Tauhid with no promised date. Points off for scope creep: Accounting also queued the rent (ticket 102's job), created an unnecessary $8 PO, and pulled 7 tools in one step. It was the most expensive run: 16 model calls, about 50k input tokens.
102 · RentAClean and cheap: 11 calls, 7 tool calls. Facilities checked the landlord's "due in 2 days" email against the lease ($2,400 due 2026-09-02). Accounting saw rent already pending as #2 and didn't create a duplicate payment, exactly the judgment you want around real money.
103 · HoodiesA−The best business decision of the run: Accounting ran check_margin at $48 (54.2%) and $44 (50.0%), checked rent before committing money, and refused a $264 restock the shop couldn't afford ($152 left, a $112 gap). The order of delegation made sense on every ticket (Inventory first for product tickets, Facilities first for rent, Customer Service last), and the Boss never called a specialist it didn't need. Where the team wasted effort was inside Accounting, not in the routing. The customer drafts were a highlight: draft #1 to Tauhid and draft #2 to the Yale AI Club were warm, short and honest about timing, and both stayed on the board unsent.

Question 2For each ticket, how did the Actual compare to the Expected plan?

  • Ticket 101: Expected and Actual lined up on the order (Inventory → Accounting → Customer Service, Facilities skipped). Two differences: the rent was queued here (#2) instead of on ticket 102, because Accounting saw rent due in 2 days while checking cash, and a $8 purchase order (#3) was created even though invoice 501 is already a "rush reprint" of this exact tee, so the PO was likely unnecessary. I approved it, which accounts for $8 of the $3,248 paid out.
  • Ticket 102: I expected a specialist-to-specialist hand-off, Facilities → Accounting. Instead the Boss routed Accounting itself, because Facilities had already reported the rent was queued. No new payment request was made, which was the right call since #2 already existed. Facilities-first matched my plan.
  • Ticket 103: Everything went as planned: Inventory first, a margin-safe discount (above 50%), no PO because cash couldn't cover it, and an honest offer of 8 now. The only minor difference: the offer quotes the price for all 20 ($960) instead of just the 8 available, though it clearly says the other 12 aren't confirmed.

My plan predicted the routing well. What it missed is that agents work across tickets: ticket 101's run changed what ticket 102 needed to do.

Question 3What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Facilities wasn't necessary in this case, though it may be for other work. On ticket 102 its whole contribution was one lookup (get_lease_rent_due(1)) plus a check of the payment list. On 101 and 103, Accounting read the rent itself. A single agent with the same MCP tools could have handled ticket 102 in about 3 tool calls instead of 11 model calls across three agents.

More generally, the specialists repeated each other's work: on ticket 101, Inventory already reported the vendor, lead time and unit cost, yet Accounting called list_vendors, get_product_pricing and get_ticket again. One agent would have kept those facts in its own context instead of re-fetching them, so the run would have been cheaper (101 used about 50k input tokens) and there'd have been no chance of scope creep like the early rent request.

What the team does buy is control: only Accounting can request money, only Customer Service can write drafts, and no agent can approve a payment. For a three-ticket shop that safety could come from tool permissions on one agent; the team becomes worth it when tickets get bigger, such as a large order, a lease renewal, or several vendors.

Question 4Three new problems this team could solve with the tools I built

1. A Crest Mug restock from a vendor that isn't blocked

CC-MUG-CREST is at 0 on hand. list_vendors shows Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead) with no open invoice, so unlike Bulldog it can ship. Accounting would price a PO with create_purchase_order_request at $3.50 each, and with only $152 left the cash check matters: 40 mugs ($140) fits, but 50 ($175) would be refused at approval.

2. Next month's rent warning

After my approval, lease 1's next_due moved to 2026-10-02, but checking holds only $152 against $2,400 rent. Facilities (get_lease_rent_due) and Accounting (get_cash_position) can flag the $2,248 shortfall well before the date and draft a heads-up to the Boss, and the pay tool would refuse any attempt to overspend.

3. A walk-in order for an item in stock, like Yale Caps

A student asks for 5 Yale Caps. check_stock shows 40 of CC-HAT-BLUE (size OS) and get_product_pricing shows a $22 list price, so Inventory confirms and Customer Service drafts a "ready for pickup" reply. No money moves, no vendor needed, and it would be the cheapest ticket the team could run.

Question 5Three new problems this team could not solve, and the tools and agents it would need

1. Taking the Yale AI Club's payment (cash coming in)

If the club accepts the $48 offer and pays $384 for the 8 hoodies on hand, there's no way to record it: cash only goes out, and the MCP server has no revenue tool. Needed: a record_sale / receive_payment tool, an orders table, and an Accounting rule that increases cash_accounts and decreases inventory together. That deposit would also fix Problem 2 above, since the shop could then cover a restock.

2. Receiving the tee shipment and holding stock

The $8 PO (#3) is due about 2026-09-05, but no tool can mark it received and add the tee to inventory, and nothing can reserve the 8 hoodies for the club so a walk-in doesn't buy them. Needed: a receive_purchase_order(request_id) tool and a reserve_stock(sku, size, qty, ticket_id) tool owned by Inventory, plus a scheduler or "advance desk date" step so arrivals can happen.

3. Actually contacting customers and vendors

Drafts #1 and #2 sit on the board forever, and nobody can ask Bulldog Print Co for a faster reprint or a payment plan. That's by design (no email or vendor calls), but a real shop would need it. Needed: a Communications agent with a send_message tool that only sends drafts a human has approved (the same pattern as approving cheques), plus a vendor inbox so replies become new tickets on the board.
